On day one I started by setting everything up, my gh repo, kicad etc, and started educating myself about pcbs and keyboard matrixes and stuff, 
then I realised that I wont be able to finish it under a day so took rest and continued my work on day two.

On day two I managed to finish with the theory, and started creating the schematic of the keyboard which I mostly finished on that day, 
leaving the rotary encoder for day three and some minor changes. This day we also started discussing some major things like, keycaps and switches, 
and after a few (tiring) hours we finally managed to settle on a nice set of red/white/black keycaps, plus we also decided that we're going to use 
either red switches or some creamier linear switches (I hope we'll manage to decide it soon..)

On day three we've finally got to have fun. We (yes WE) started designing our pcb! I finished the schematic and moved on to the pcb where 
I arranged the switches and my gf, ms helpful, arranged the diodes to the switches, then she even started to wire things up, but she's got tired too so we finished the F.cu layer and called it a day.

I hope next day I'll be able to finish the KiCAD part and I'll be able to move onto the 3D designing part, which I'm familiar with a bit cause I've already designed some stuff on TinkerCAD, but I'm planning to design this in Fusion.

On day 4, we finally finished making our own pcb, and ms.artist sketched down the design of the case. We considered several options, but at last we 
agreed on a design, which has an about 5 degrees drop, with sharp edges, excluding the need of those little adjustable legs which always fall off, and we chose an integrated plate design.

On days 5 and 6, we made the 3d design of the case using the program Fusion. It did cause us some trouble at the beginning, (in means of that my pc almost crashed, the program crashed 
which made us have to redo the case all over again) but soon enough, we started to feel familiar with the mechanics. And after a lot of suffering, (fine not a lot, but it did feel like an eternity) the design was finished at last.

day 7, I'm splitting our design so every part would fit on a 200x200 buildplate, while my girlfriend makes the BOM and we plan to commit everything and submit our project!

This journey has been educational for both of us. I finally managed to design something with a software that is not TinkerCAD, I also learned how 
to design a PCB(using KiCAD obviously) while my girlfriend could see the process of creating something with our own hands and not just lifting it from a shelf. 
She also tried pcb and 3D designing and she helped me with her goofy sketches. With the knowledge aquired here, we both look forward to start working on bigger projects of our own.

Thank you for following us along, and for reading! :)
